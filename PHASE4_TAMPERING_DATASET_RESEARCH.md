# Phase 4: Kaggle QR Visual-Tampering Dataset Research

**Conclusion:** I could not verify a publicly available Kaggle dataset that labels visual QR authenticity/tampering, sticker replacement, or original-versus-manipulated QR pairs. The QR datasets below are relevant adjacent resources, but their published semantics do not establish those labels. None was downloaded; project files and datasets were not changed.

Research checked Kaggle dataset cards/metadata and available file-list summaries on 4 October 2026. Kaggle dataset pages were directly reviewed where their public metadata was available. A dataset's title or Kaggle category was not treated as evidence of tampering labels.

## Assessment

None of the verified Kaggle QR image datasets provides labels for a clean QR versus a physically replaced/sticker-overlaid/manipulated QR image. The two datasets explicitly about QR images are for URL reputation, QR/barcode detection, or finder-pattern localization. Their image-level categories cannot be repurposed as tampering labels without creating unsupported labels.

The general CG1050 Kaggle dataset does have original/tampered image categories, but it is not QR-specific and the public card does not document QR content or a row-level pair manifest for its v2 crops. It is not a defensible substitute for QR-tampering data.

**Recommendation:** Do not use any of these Kaggle datasets to claim supervised visual QR-tampering detection. Continue searching for a dataset with documented QR-specific manipulations and provenance (for example, genuine sticker-overlaid/replacement captures or controlled manipulated images), explicit class definitions, and original-to-manipulated parent IDs if pairing is needed. A model trained on URL-labeled QR images would answer a different security question.

## Evidence and limitations

- [Kaggle card: Benign and Malicious QR codes](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/samahsadiq/benign-and-malicious-qr-codes) — official public metadata describes the encoded URL labels, 200,000 balanced images, and unknown license.
- [Kaggle card: Barcode & QR Codes](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jonathanimmanuel/barcode-and-qr) — official public metadata describes barcode/QR object recognition, varied conditions, detection labels, total bytes, and CC BY 4.0.
- [Kaggle card: Finder Patterns (QR code)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/samygrisard/finder-patterns-qr-code) — official public metadata gives the image count, finder-pattern/Yolo annotation task, bytes, and unknown license; [the derived QR detector repository](https://github.com/kolabit/qr-codes) corroborates its detection purpose.
- [Kaggle card: Original and Tampered Image Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/saurabhshahane/cg1050) — official public page describes class folders, cropped manipulation regions, 2,088 files, 132.71 MB, and CC BY 4.0.

Some Kaggle Data Explorer details were not rendered in the public page interface. In particular, the total image count and full annotation schema for `Barcode & QR Codes`, and any stable v2 image-pair mapping for CG1050, remain unverified. Dataset licenses shown on Kaggle should still be read alongside source/provenance terms; a platform listing alone does not resolve rights in third-party image content.
